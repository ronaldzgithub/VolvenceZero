Brain-inspired visual sensing–perception for open-world environments | Nature Sensors

## Abstract

Robust visual perception in open-world environments remains a fundamental challenge, as data representations degrade under extreme imaging conditions. By contrast, the human visual system maintains resilience through compositional representations built from visual primitives. Here we introduce a brain-inspired two-stage learning framework for adaptive and robust perception in open-world settings. In the bottom-up stage, structured visual priors are embedded into intermediate representations, enabling self-supervised learning of an internal model that fuses complementary cues. In the top-down stage, distilled visual knowledge from this model adaptively modulates downstream perception algorithms to enhance robustness and generalization. We instantiate the framework using the brain-inspired vision sensor Tianmouc and evaluate it on datasets capturing real-world extreme scenarios. Experiments show that bottom-up learning yields consistent generalization across diverse degradations, while top-down modulation substantially improves monocular depth estimation and video instance segmentation under severe interference. These results establish a principled brain-inspired computational approach for advancing artificial visual intelligence and understanding human vision.

## Extended data

### Extended Data Fig. 1 The sampling principle of Tianmouc and the comparison with event-based vision sensors (EVSs).

a, through hybrid pixel array design and circuit design, Tianmouc enables complete simultaneous output of three-modal data. b, The raw data and the reconstruction results of different cameras. Green represents positive values and blue represents negative values. For both the TD and the SD, brightness indicates magnitude.

### Extended Data Fig. 2 Construction process and algorithm of IR.

a, three IR construction methods: HDR fusion based on the 2-D Poisson equation, optical-flow-based warping to encode motion, and direct accumulation of spatiotemporal variations with color frame. b, UNet network used for \({F}_{I}\), which directly receives RGB, TD, and SD data from 0 to \({t}_{f}\), and output the RGB at time \({t}_{f}\). c, SD-RAFT is proposed to estimate optical flow for \({F}_{O}\).

### Extended Data Fig. 3 Fundamentals of the brain-inspired visual learning framework.

a, taking the fusion of three IRs as an example, to obtain the optimal result, estimated VGT, requires the guarantee of multiple mechanisms. b, according to the framework, we develop a 2-step self-supervised training scheme. Different IRs are firstly built using Tianmouc data, then the internal models are trained with a symmetric self-supervised learning process. c, Details of the IGFNet structure, where IGFNet is a dedicated implementation of an internal model.

### Extended Data Fig. 4 VGT estimation on different corner cases.

For the e-VGT task, we compared the real-world inference performance of different baselines under various corner cases, and IGFNet demonstrates the best robustness.

### Extended Data Fig. 5 Comparison results between the DAM-V2-L model on Tianmouc real-captured data and the depth provided by the RGB-D sensor.

DAMv2 exhibits strong generalization ability. Without fine-tuning, it can produce high-precision, scale-aligned depth results from e-VGT that match real-world depth scales, making it suitable to serve as our depth SGT.

### Extended Data Fig. 6 Video instance segmentation annotation process.

a, Overall data annotation pipeline b, Annotation based on video object tracking methods and image instance segmentation. The video object segmentation uses the XMem model, and the instances and their masks are provided by mask2former. Key parts include the merging and repair of masks.

### Extended Data Fig. 7 Class distribution and annotation quality of the Tianmouc-VIS dataset.

a, Class distribution of the Tianmouc-VIS samples b, Annotation on e-VGT and RGB. The annotations based on original RGB images are also placed on the top of the e-VGT to highlight the errors, which is substantially mitigated by labeling on the e-VGT.

### Extended Data Fig. 8 Neural network structural details of the VIS perception algorithms.

By modifying the HDR branch as well as the fusion layer, we construct different perceptual algorithms to achieve a trade-off between accuracy and speed.
